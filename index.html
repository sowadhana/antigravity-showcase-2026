The S$150,000 Piece of Paper: Inside Singapore’s Great Vehicle Quota Squeeze

How statutory zero vehicle growth, EV tax incentives, and auction dynamics triggered the world's most aggressive mobility market—driving Category A mass-market licenses to an all-time peak of S$139,001.
By Sowadhana, Senior Investigative Data Fellow
SINGAPORE — Updated Nov. 23, 2026

In an island nation where urban planners can boast the world’s most seamless rail network, the certificate granting the simple right to own an automobile has officially disconnected from everyday purchasing power. Formally established in May 1990 as an auction-driven deterrent to traffic paralysis, the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) has evolved into Singapore's most consequential macroeconomic scarcity instrument.

Behind these extraordinary premiums lies a statutory feedback loop. Since February 2018, Singapore has mandated an absolute Zero Vehicle Growth policy for passenger cars. For a new vehicle to enter circulation, another must be scrapped or de-registered. When de-registrations collapsed to historical lows between 2021 and 2023, available bidding quotas fell off a mathematical cliff. By October 2023, Open Category E premiums reached an all-time high of S$158,004, and Category B touched S$150,001.

Latest Bidding Round: 2026-11 (Round 2) Benchmark Quotas, Clearing Premiums & Historical Peaks

Yet the most startling structural shift in our sixteen-year dataset is not the rise of luxury car premiums, but the complete inversion of the mass-market boundary. Category A—nominally intended for entry-level family cars with engines up to 1,600cc and 130 horsepower—has experienced a dramatic downmarket squeeze. In November 2026 Round 1, Category A premiums surged to an unprecedented all-time record of S$139,001, surpassing Category B by S$6,667.

This market inversion is driven by a convergence of electric vehicle incentives and commercial fleet expansion. As automobile manufacturers specifically de-tune electric drivetrains (such as BYD, Hyundai, and Tesla) to slip under the 110 kW (147 hp) Category A threshold before Singapore's EV Early Adoption Incentives step down, affluent households and ride-hailing operators have invaded Category A auctions, crowding out middle-class car buyers.

The relationship between quota supply and bidding prices reveals the severe price elasticity inherent in sealed-bid uniform pricing. When bi-weekly Category A quota supply is restricted beneath 600 certificates, the bid-to-quota ratio routinely spikes past 1.6×, triggering panic bidding across automotive dealerships with backlogged sales orders.

“Under zero vehicle growth, auctions do not calculate the cost of a car; they calculate the monetary threshold of Singapore's wealthiest decile.”

Conversely, cross-referencing our bidding ledger against SingStat’s Motor Vehicle Retail Sales Index illustrates that overall automotive sales volume contracts sharply during peak COE regimes, even as aggregate dollar expenditure remains buoyant due to inflated sticker prices.
